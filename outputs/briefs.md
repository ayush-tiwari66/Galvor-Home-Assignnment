# Creator briefs

One brief per selected creator. Each was drafted with an LLM from checked evidence and passed the validator in `lj.briefs`: every post exists and belongs to that creator, every ad exists, a cited ad shows a child enjoying the same product line, claims come only from the claims library, and every number is filled in by code.

**Every product claim needs brand and legal approval before use.** The library holds only what past ads have said, not what is approved.

## `the_mum_wife_playbook`: Nutrimix 2+

**The idea:** A playful mother-daughter skit in which Seerat keeps demanding "woh wala doodh" and visibly enjoys it, built on the repetitive back-and-forth humour the feed already runs on.

**Why this creator**

- Already shows their daughter enjoying a food product on camera, and child-parent comic dialogue is their native format. ([post](https://www.instagram.com/p/DeYypNeTkm8/), [post](https://www.instagram.com/p/DeJUa9bsS-K/), [post](https://www.instagram.com/p/DeEGnivMrj_/))
- Their daughter is in 74% of recent posts, so the child-enjoying moment won't feel staged. ([post](https://www.instagram.com/p/Ddq6c6-Mbk3/), [post](https://www.instagram.com/p/DeBNEtpMaw9/))
- Paid posts don't lose the audience here: sponsored videos got 2.3 times the plays of organic ones, and only 21% of the feed is sponsored. ([post](https://www.instagram.com/p/DeOEO3NThTK/))

**What to say**

- **Opening:** Seerat on screen in the first second, repeating her demand to Mumma, with a text overlay setting up the joke.
- **Reason to care:** Picky about plain milk
- **The proof moment:** Seerat tastes the milk and enjoys it on camera. Let the reaction be real; don't give the child lines about nutrition.
- **The ask:** Buy now, through the link or the creator's code
- **Tone:** Hinglish, handheld, natural home light, text overlays for the punchline.

**Running order**

1. Seerat asks for "woh wala doodh" again and again; Mumma pretends not to understand. Keep it in their usual comic rhythm.
2. Mumma offers plain milk and Seerat pushes it away. This is the picky-milk problem, shown rather than explained.
3. Mumma stirs in Nutrimix. Seerat tastes it, reacts and drinks it down. Hold on this moment: it is the proof.
4. Mumma to camera, in their own words, says why they're comfortable giving it, using only approved claims.
5. Clear ask: tap the link to try it.

## How to test each brief

- **Paid test budget:** about ₹23,000 per creator in partnership-ad spend, on top of the creator's fee. At the brand's median cost per purchase in kids' ads (₹543) that buys roughly 42 purchases, and even at the upper-quartile cost it reaches 30, the point where this project treats a result as readable.
- **Early check at ₹12,000:** stop if both hook rate is below 23.9% and click-through is below 0.7% (the bottom quarter of the brand's kids' ads on both). Fix the opening before spending more.
- **At ₹23,000:** scale if ROAS is at or above 1.08 and conversion at or above 3.0% (the brand's medians). Stop if ROAS is below 0.82 (bottom quarter). In between, try a second cut before deciding.
- **Compare like with like:** run one version with the child-enjoying moment and, where the creator agrees, one without it. That tells the brand whether the pattern in its old ads holds for creators.
- These benchmarks come from the brand's ad export, whose numbers are made up for this exercise. Replace them with real account benchmarks before use.
