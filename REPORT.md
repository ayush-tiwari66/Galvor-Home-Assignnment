*For the marketing manager. Prepared 5 Nov 2026 from the brand's ad export (100 ads) and 392 recent posts by the 20 shortlisted creators.*

> The ad performance figures in the export were made up for this exercise. Every number below about ad results describes that export, not Little Joys' real results. The method carries over; the numbers would need re-running on the real account.

## Our verdict on creators who worked with competitors

**Recommendation:** for this first round, don't sign anyone who did a paid post for a kids' nutrition rival in the last 90 days. Before signing, call the two strongest of them to check whether they're free.

Six of the 20 creators did paid work for a direct rival in the last 90 days. We checked whether any of it was a passing mention that could be let through. None was: all were paid partnerships, and every product does the same job as a Little Joys product. Gritzo, Bournvita and Complan are shown going into a child's milk, like Nutrimix. Troovy is pitched as the answer for a child who refuses milk and breakfast, which is also Nutrimix's job. Good Monk goes into roti dough, like Proteinmix.

This is a business call, so here is what each policy would mean:

| Policy | Top five |
|---|---|
| **Strict, recommended:** any recent rival rules a creator out | `the_mum_wife_playbook`, `hello_ps8`, `ohmother.in`, `mumma_and_ridhu`, `foodies_by_neha` |
| Rule out challenger brands only (Gritzo, Troovy, Good Monk); allow mass brands (Bournvita, Complan) | `instamommy_suchi`, `the_mum_wife_playbook`, `hello_ps8`, `ohmother.in`, `mumma_and_ridhu` |
| Rule nobody out; just flag the risk | `sia_3vedi`, `raja_choudhary04`, `instamommy_suchi`, `the_mum_wife_playbook`, `doc_mama_and_babies` |

Why strict:
- We can't see anyone's contract. Category exclusivity clauses are common in paid deals; that's general industry practice, not something this data shows.
- Their audiences have just heard them recommend a rival product for the same job.
- The mass-brand exception lets in exactly one creator, `instamommy_suchi`. That creator put Bournvita and then Complan into their child's milk within ten weeks, the last time on 6 October. Three-quarters of their feed is paid posts. A Little Joys post next would be the least believable recommendation on the list.

**Make two phone calls first.** `sia_3vedi` and `raja_choudhary04` are the strongest fits in the whole file. Both have children the right age, have already filmed a child enjoying a food product, and have repeatable reach. If either confirms they're free of category exclusivity and is willing to leave a gap after the rival work, swap them in for `foodies_by_neha` and `mumma_and_ridhu`, the two weakest picks.

One more worth a look: **`storiesbyvihaa`** already lists Little Joys among their past-year brands, so you have your own results from working with them. We left them out because their measured audience is 70% men, mostly 18 to 34, which isn't evidence of parents buying. If your past results with them were good, that outweighs our concern.

## What every creator should say

These four rules come from the brand's own ads. Near-copies of the same ad were counted once, and the one very large ad (LJ-004) was set aside so it couldn't drive the results.

1. **Show a child visibly enjoying the product.** This is the strongest sales signal in the file. Ads with a child tasting the product returned ₹1.78 per ₹1 spent, against ₹0.93 for the rest. It still held after removing the biggest ads and controlling for product and language. Every brief is built around this moment.
2. **End with a clear ask.** Ads ending in "buy now" converted 3.3% of clicks against 2.2% for the rest. Asking costs nothing.
3. **Keep the parent's story and the popular angles, but pair them with the child moment.** Parent-led ads did better with a child enjoying the product (₹1.35 against ₹0.97). Picky-eating ads did too (₹1.65 against ₹0.83). Picky eating, recipes and growth work as the reason to care, not as the proof.
4. **Don't pick creators or openings for attention alone.** A child in the first three seconds got more people watching (35.5% against 26.7%) but no more purchases once they clicked. The brand's spend followed attention, not return.

These are associations in one ad file, not proof of cause. Nothing reached our top evidence grade; these are the patterns worth testing first. The details are in [outputs/ad_patterns.md](outputs/ad_patterns.md).

## How the five were chosen

1. **Basic checks on all 20.** Children on camera must be old enough for the product, the feed must be about family or food, and the creator must have posted in the last 90 days. Ten failed: seven mostly show babies under 2, one shows only teenagers, one is a comedy account with no children, and one has a measured audience that is mostly men.
2. **The strict competitor rule** removed four more, leaving six.
3. **A score out of 100.** The most weight (30%) goes to whether the creator can naturally show a child enjoying the product. The rest covers age and food fit, typical organic reach, how full the feed already is with ads, and whether the profile matches the posts.
4. **Stress-testing the weights.** We re-ran the ranking under 5,000 random weightings and six named ones. The first four picks hold under every named weighting. Slot 5 swings between `foodies_by_neha` and `radhikabansal0908`, whose feed is 100% paid posts, so treat slot 5 as the weakest. The score barely tracks follower count (rank correlation +0.07).

All 20 creators, with the reason for each exclusion: [outputs/creator_rankings.md](outputs/creator_rankings.md).

## How sure we are

- **Nothing in the data links a creator to a sale**, so the score measures fit, not future sales. That's why each pick is a test.
- **Three of the five are Low confidence**, mostly because of small reach or thin evidence. Audience data exists for only 3 of the 20 creators, so ask each one for an Instagram audience screenshot.
- **Labels were checked, not assumed.** We compared 20 ad videos with the system's labels. The child labels held up; the product label didn't, so we replaced it with a hand-reviewed map of all 100 ads.
- **Some signals were left out on purpose.** Likes are hidden on some accounts and comments are inflated or switched off, so typical plays are the only engagement signal used. The labels for how early the brand is named contradict each other, so they weren't used.
- **Claims aren't approved.** Briefs use only claims past ads have made, and each needs brand and legal sign-off. Some ads say both "zero added sugar" and "sweetened with jaggery and dates", so the briefs avoid the sugar claim.

## How to run it

For each creator, put about **₹23,000** of paid spend behind the post as a partnership ad, on top of their fee. That figure comes from the cost per purchase in the brand's own kids' ads. Even at the higher end of those costs it reaches about 30 purchases, the point where a result starts to mean something.

| When | Stop if | Scale if |
|---|---|---|
| At ₹12,000 | Hook rate below 23.9% **and** click-through below 0.7% (both in the brand's bottom quarter) | |
| At ₹23,000 | Return below ₹0.82 per ₹1 (bottom quarter) | Return at or above ₹1.08 per ₹1 and conversion at or above 3.0% (the brand's medians) |

Where a creator agrees, also run a version without the child-enjoying moment. That tells you directly whether rule 1 holds for creator content, which is the biggest open question here.
